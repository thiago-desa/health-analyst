**This example is entirely fictional.** It is not a real patient, not a record of a live run, and not medical advice, a diagnosis, or a treatment plan. Names, dates, lab values, and genetic statements below were invented for documentation.

## What this might mean (hypotheses, not a diagnosis)

1. **Thyroid pattern.** A TSH above the lab range with a free T4 still inside range is often discussed as a subclinical hypothyroid pattern. It is not, on its own, a diagnosis, and a clinician may want a repeat test, antibodies, or a symptom review before deciding anything. No thyroid medication should be started or stopped from this read.
2. **Glucose line.** A single fasting glucose just over this lab's cut-off is a reason to ask about a repeat fasting glucose or an HbA1c, which was not in the packet. It is not enough, here, to label diabetes.
3. **Lynch panel limit.** "Variant not detected" on a short consumer list does not rule Lynch syndrome out. A clinician or genetics service would decide whether personal or family history (not provided in this fiction) warrants a different test.

No finding in this fiction is an emergency on its own. Sudden severe chest pain, one-sided weakness, or trouble speaking would still be a reason to seek emergency care, even if a recent checkup looked mild.

## Questions to take to a clinician

- Whether to repeat TSH and free T4, and whether thyroid antibodies would help.
- Whether to add or repeat a glycemic test, given the single fasting glucose.
- Whether family or personal history would change colorectal or genetic-testing plans, given the limited consumer panel.
